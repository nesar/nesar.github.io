Foundation Models

Foundation Models research, exemplified by the AstroMLab series and EAIRA, targets developing and rigorously evaluating domain-specialized AI for scientific applications. Key methods include fine-tuning large language models (LLMs), ranging from 8B to 70B parameters, with domain-specific data to achieve high-fidelity reasoning and question-answering. This specialization yields benchmark-topping performance, often surpassing generalist models like GPT-4o in complex tasks. Concurrently, robust evaluation methodologies are established to assess AI's utility as scientific research assistants. The collective impact demonstrates a powerful paradigm for accelerating scientific discovery through efficient, domain-optimized AI, enabling sophisticated automated analysis.

Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning increasingly underpins scientific discovery and data analysis. Deep learning, particularly neural networks and generative adversarial networks, is pivotal for astronomical applications, including point spread function deconvolution, anomaly detection in galaxy images, and automated gravitational lens detection and modeling. Unsupervised methods and probabilistic modeling explore galaxy morphology beyond traditional classifications and reconstruct global fields from sparse sensor data. A key focus is enhancing model interpretability through disentangled latent spaces and physical benchmarking of AI-generated scientific data, such as the cosmic web. This research leverages advanced ML techniques to automate complex analyses, uncover hidden patterns, and improve the quality and understanding of scientific datasets.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Dark Matter and Cosmology research employs diverse methodologies to unveil the universe's structure and its enigmatic constituents. Key approaches include using cosmic shear analysis of survey data, like Hyper Suprime-Cam, to constrain modified gravity theories. The large-scale cosmic web is explored through its geometry and topology using multistream phase-space views and N-body simulations, revealing fine-grained details and caustics within dark matter halos. Observational programs, such as SPTpol cluster surveys and Gaia-based stellar mapping of the Milky Way using red clump and Carbon-Enhanced Metal-Poor stars, provide crucial tracers for mass distribution and the evolution of cosmic structures. These efforts collectively refine our understanding of dark matter's nature and the Universe's fundamental laws.

Emulation & Inference

Emulation and Inference research leverages advanced machine learning, including probabilistic and deep neural networks, alongside Gaussian process emulation, to accelerate scientific discovery across diverse fields. Key methods involve developing fast surrogate models for complex systems like fluid flows and cosmological large-scale structure, drastically reducing computational costs. These techniques also enable robust inference of physical parameters, such as galaxy cluster masses, cosmic redshifts, and peculiar velocities, from observational data. A central focus is interpretable uncertainty quantification, ensuring reliable and trustworthy predictions. This work significantly enhances computational efficiency, allowing for deeper exploration of physical theories, improving data recovery, and providing more accurate scientific insights.
