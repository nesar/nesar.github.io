Astronomical Data Analysis with AI/ML

Nesar S Ramachandra's research significantly advances astronomical data analysis through innovative AI/ML applications. Neural Networks are employed for Point Spread Function deconvolution, substantially improving image quality. Machine learning techniques leverage synthetic spectra for probabilistic redshift estimation, enhancing cosmological measurements. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are robustly applied to detect anomalies in galaxy images, enabling the discovery of novel celestial phenomena. Unsupervised machine learning explores galaxy morphology beyond traditional classifications, deepening our understanding of galaxy evolution. Furthermore, a modular deep learning pipeline automates the detection and modeling of strong gravitational lenses, providing crucial insights into dark matter distribution and cosmology. This work streamlines complex analysis, accelerating astronomical discovery.

Galactic & Stellar Astrophysics

Nesar S Ramachandra's research significantly contributes to understanding the Milky Way's structure and early chemical evolution. One study establishes an unprecedented photometric sample of 2.6 million Red Clump (RC) stars, spanning the inner to outer Galaxy. Utilizing RC stars as robust standard candles, this work provides critical constraints for mapping galactic architecture and age-metallicity relations. Concurrently, another key paper identifies Carbon-Enhanced Metal-Poor (CEMP) star candidates from Gaia DR3 BP/RP spectra. These ancient stars are crucial probes of primordial star formation and early universe nucleosynthesis. Together, this research leverages massive stellar surveys and spectral analysis to illuminate both the contemporary structure and the earliest chemical enrichment history of our Galaxy.
