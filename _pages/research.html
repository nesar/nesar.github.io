Foundation Models

Recent Foundation Model (FM) research significantly advances their application in scientific domains, particularly astronomy and cosmology. Key methodologies involve developing specialized Large Language Models (LLMs) and multi-modal FMs, often with parameter scales from 8B to 70B. These models are meticulously fine-tuned to acquire domain-specific expertise, enabling them to interpret complex cosmological data and understand fields like spectroscopy. The impact is profound: these specialized FMs achieve benchmark-topping and even GPT-4o level performance in astronomy Q&A and serve as smart assistants for data analysis. Concurrently, new methodologies are established to rigorously evaluate AI models as scientific research assistants. This research positions FMs as powerful, domain-specialized tools poised to accelerate scientific discovery and enhance research productivity.

Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning is profoundly impacting scientific research, particularly in astrophysics. Advanced ML methods, including neural networks, generative models, and probabilistic frameworks, tackle challenges like sparse data, anomaly detection, and high-dimensional analysis. Key applications involve robust galaxy morphology classification, precise redshift and peculiar velocity estimation, strong gravitational lens detection, and reconstructing cosmic structures. A critical focus is on enhancing interpretability and uncertainty quantification through statistically disentangled latent spaces and rigorous error estimation. These developments are vital for validating AI-evolved simulations, reducing model error, and fostering new discoveries across complex scientific datasets.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Dark Matter & Cosmology research employs diverse methodologies to probe the universe's fundamental constituents and structure. Cosmic shear analysis of Hyper Suprime-Cam data constrains modified gravity and dark energy theories. Advanced cosmological simulations, including CRK-HACC and multistream views, reveal the fine-grained structure and dynamics of dark matter haloes and the cosmic web, elucidating caustic and topological features. Observational efforts leverage photometric galaxy samples (e.g., Red Clump stars for Galactic structure) and wide-field surveys like SPTpol's extended cluster survey. Future missions like SPHEREx will provide spectroscopic maps to further trace the cosmic web and constrain cosmological parameters, collectively advancing our knowledge of dark matter's role and cosmic evolution.

Emulation & Inference

Emulation and inference research leverages advanced machine learning techniques to address computationally intensive scientific problems across diverse fields. Key methods include neural network-based emulators, probabilistic neural networks (PNNs), and Gaussian process (GP) emulation. These surrogates are often integrated with reduced-order models (ROMs) to efficiently capture complex system dynamics, including latent-space time evolution. Applications range from accelerating cosmological subgrid model and modified gravity inferences to developing robust, uncertainty-aware surrogates for fluid flows and enabling data recovery. The impact is profound, allowing for rapid parameter estimation, real-time predictions, and reliable uncertainty quantification, thereby transforming scientific discovery and engineering design by overcoming computational bottlenecks.
