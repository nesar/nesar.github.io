Foundation Models

Foundation Model research is revolutionizing scientific inquiry, especially in astronomy and cosmology. Key methods center on domain-specialization, adapting large language models (LLMs) to interpret complex scientific data. Efforts include multi-modal foundation models for cosmological simulations and training LLMs to "speak spectroscopy." The AstroMLab series demonstrates specialized LLMs achieving benchmark-topping performance in astronomy Q&A, even rivaling general-purpose models like GPT-4o with fewer parameters. This approach fosters sophisticated scientific reasoning, efficient knowledge retrieval, and advanced data analysis, fundamentally accelerating discovery across astrophysics through deep domain understanding.

Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning (ML) is revolutionizing scientific research, especially in astrophysics and cosmology. Applications span generative modeling and deep neural networks for tasks like galaxy morphology classification, strong gravitational lens detection, point spread function deconvolution, and probabilistic redshift estimation. ML also addresses challenges of sparse, high-dimensional data, enabling global field reconstruction and anomaly detection in large datasets. A core focus is enhancing interpretability and rigorously quantifying uncertainty to foster scientific trustworthiness. Additionally, robust methodologies are emerging for benchmarking AI-evolved simulations and evaluating ML as intelligent scientific assistants, integrating these tools into modular pipelines for advanced data analysis and discovery.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Recent Dark Matter and Cosmology research utilizes diverse methods to unravel the universe's structure and fundamental physics. Cosmological simulations, particularly those adopting a multi-stream view, characterize the intricate topology and geometry of the dark matter cosmic web and its caustics, informing galaxy formation models. Weak lensing techniques, including cosmic shear analyses of large surveys like Hyper Suprime-Cam, constrain modified gravity theories and precisely estimate galaxy cluster masses by optimizing galaxy selection. Future missions like SPHEREx promise expanded data. Additionally, extensive stellar samples from Gaia and other surveys trace galactic structures and probe early universe conditions, collectively advancing our understanding of dark matter distribution and the universe's evolution.

Emulation & Inference

Emulation and inference research extensively employs machine learning, primarily neural networks and Gaussian processes, to develop efficient surrogates for computationally intensive physical simulations. This methodology finds critical application in cosmology, facilitating inference of subgrid models, predictions for large-scale structure, and modeling of modified gravity theories. Furthermore, it addresses fluid dynamics for surrogate modeling, data recovery, and reduced-order representations. The key impact lies in enabling rapid parameter inference, providing differentiable predictions for optimization, and rigorously quantifying uncertainties through probabilistic approaches. These techniques significantly accelerate scientific discovery by transforming complex computational tasks into fast, accurate, and interpretable models.
