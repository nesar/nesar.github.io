Foundation Models

Foundation Models research explores developing specialized AI for scientific inquiry, exemplified by the AstroMLab series in astronomy. Initial work established the potential of Large Language Models (LLMs) in domain-specific question-answering. Subsequent advancements demonstrated significant performance gains through domain adaptation, with specialized 8B-parameter models achieving GPT-4o level capabilities and 70B-parameter reasoning models setting new benchmarks in astronomy Q&A. These efforts highlight the efficacy of tailored architectures and increased scale for achieving superior domain-specific performance. Concurrently, methodologies like EAIRA are being developed to rigorously evaluate AI models as scientific research assistants, crucial for their reliable integration and utility across diverse research applications.

Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning is profoundly impacting scientific research, especially in astronomy and physics. Key methods include generative models, like GANs, which are widely employed for anomaly detection in astronomical images, enabling the discovery of rare phenomena. These models also facilitate generating and physically benchmarking synthetic scientific data, such as the cosmic web, accelerating complex simulations. Deep learning pipelines enhance image processing tasks, like point spread function deconvolution, improving data quality and resolution. Unsupervised machine learning methods are further applied to explore complex data, for instance, extending galaxy morphology classification beyond traditional schemes. Moreover, deep learning aids in global field reconstruction from sparse sensors and automates the detection and modeling of strong gravitational lenses, significantly advancing our understanding of the universe.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Dark Matter and Cosmology research employs diverse methods to unravel cosmic structure. Cosmic shear analysis of survey data (e.g., Hyper Suprime-Cam) constrains modified gravity and dark energy. Sunyaev-Zel'dovich effect cluster surveys (e.g., SPTpol) identify massive dark matter halos, vital for cosmological parameters. Numerical simulations, using "multistream" techniques, precisely map the dark matter cosmic web's topology, geometry, and internal halo caustics. Further, stellar populations like Milky Way Red Clump stars or Gaia DR3 metal-poor candidates trace galactic dark matter distributions. These integrated observational and simulation-based approaches critically advance our understanding of cosmic evolution and dark matter's fundamental role.

Emulation & Inference

Emulation and inference research leverages advanced machine learning, including deep and probabilistic neural networks and Gaussian processes, to develop high-fidelity surrogate models for computationally intensive simulations. This approach tackles diverse problems in astrophysics (e.g., weak lensing, large-scale structure, peculiar velocities, redshift estimation) and fluid dynamics, often through reduced-order modeling. A paramount focus is interpretable uncertainty quantification, providing robust probabilistic predictions and parameter inference. By significantly reducing computational cost and model error, these methods accelerate scientific discovery, enable efficient exploration of complex parameter spaces, and enhance the reliability and trustworthiness of AI-driven scientific predictions.
