Foundation Models

Foundation Models (FMs) are transforming scientific research. Methods focus on specializing multi-modal FMs and Large Language Models (LLMs) for domain-specific tasks, including interpreting cosmological simulations, spectroscopic data, and complex ensembles. Approaches involve fine-tuning LLMs to "speak spectroscopy" and developing specialized models achieving GPT-4o level performance efficiently. Agentic AI systems are also emerging for autonomous discovery. The impact is significant: FMs act as intelligent research assistants, delivering benchmark-topping Q&A, accelerating data analysis, and aiding discovery across scales from quarks to cosmos. Rigorous evaluation methodologies ensure their scientific utility.

Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning methods are transforming scientific research, notably in astronomy. Deep learning and generative adversarial networks (GANs) excel in tasks like anomaly detection in astronomical images, point spread function deconvolution, and classifying complex galaxy morphologies, advancing beyond traditional approaches. These techniques facilitate the analysis of vast datasets, from identifying rare stellar populations in Gaia DR3 to benchmarking AI-evolved cosmological structure formation. Probabilistic modeling and unsupervised learning further enable the discovery of new phenomena and the construction of robust models for high-dimensional data. Emphasis is placed on developing interpretable and physically informed ML frameworks, ensuring impactful scientific insights across stellar, galactic, and cosmological scales.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Recent dark matter and cosmology research utilizes diverse methods to probe cosmic structure and fundamental physics. Cosmological simulations, exemplified by CRK-HACC, model galaxy formation and the intricate dark matter web, analyzing its topology, geometry, and multistream internal dynamics. Observational efforts include SPHEREx, mapping large-scale structure with infrared spectroscopy, and SPTpol cluster surveys, identifying massive structures. Crucially, cosmic shear analyses, using Hyper Suprime-Cam data, constrain modified gravity theories like f(R). These integrated approaches provide critical insights into dark matter distribution, the cosmic web's detailed design, and the fundamental forces shaping the universe.

Emulation & Inference

Research in emulation and inference employs advanced machine learning, including deep and probabilistic neural networks, to accelerate scientific discovery. Key methods involve developing fast surrogates and reduced-order models for complex simulations like cosmological matter power spectra or fluid dynamics. A central focus is interpretable uncertainty quantification, often via probabilistic frameworks and Gaussian process emulation, ensuring robust predictions. This enables efficient parameter space exploration, improved inference from sparse data (e.g., redshift, weak lensing, peculiar velocities), and enhanced data recovery. These advancements significantly reduce model error and accelerate scientific analysis in fields like cosmology and fluid dynamics.
