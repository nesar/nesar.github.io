Foundation Models

Research on Foundation Models for scientific domains highlights the efficacy of specialization and rigorous evaluation. The AstroMLab series showcases advanced methodologies in astronomy, demonstrating that domain-specialized large language models achieve benchmark-topping performance in complex Q&A. Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning is transforming scientific research by extracting insights from complex, high-dimensional data. Core methods include generative models, such as GANs, employed for anomaly detection and physical simulations, with efforts on interpretability through disentangled latent spaces. Deep learning pipelines address tasks like image deconvolution, object detection, and global field reconstruction from sparse sensors. Unsupervised techniques facilitate novel data exploration, such as galaxy morphology analysis. Crucially, research emphasizes interpretable uncertainty quantification and probabilistic modeling across diverse scientific domains, fostering robust discovery and enhancing trust in AI-driven insights.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Dark Matter and Cosmology research increasingly employs diverse methods to constrain fundamental physics and characterize the Universe's structure. Weak lensing analyses, like those using Hyper Suprime-Cam data, probe modified gravity and estimate galaxy cluster masses, crucial for cosmological constraints. Sunyaev-Zel'dovich surveys further identify clusters, mapping large-scale structure. Complementary theoretical work utilizes multi-stream phase space analysis and simulations to detail the dark matter web, revealing the topological and geometric intricacies of halos and filaments, including caustic formation. These advanced techniques provide unprecedented insights into dark matter distribution from galactic scales to the cosmic web, refining our understanding of cosmic evolution.

Emulation & Inference

Emulation and inference research employs advanced machine learning, including probabilistic and deep neural networks, reduced-order models, and Gaussian process emulation, to accelerate scientific discovery. Applications span astrophysics—from large scale structure and redshift predictions to peculiar velocity estimation and matter power spectrum emulation for modified gravity cosmologies—and fluid dynamics for fast surrogates and data recovery. This paradigm delivers rapid, accurate, and often differentiable predictions. Crucially, it quantifies uncertainty, significantly reduces computational costs, and facilitates robust scientific inference across complex physical systems.
