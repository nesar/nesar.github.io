Dark Matter & Cosmology

The research area of Dark Matter and Cosmology critically investigates the invisible matter component dominating the Universe’s mass budget and its role in shaping the large-scale cosmic web and the formation of dark matter halos. Understanding the complex, non-linear gravitational evolution of dark matter remains a paramount challenge. Dr. Nesar Ramachandra’s contributions significantly advance this field by pioneering a "multi-stream" paradigm to describe the intricate dynamics of dark matter structures. This innovative approach moves beyond single-fluid approximations, offering a richer portrait of the cosmic web and the internal structure of dark matter halos by considering the superposition of multiple dark matter flows. Specifically, the research characterizes the dark matter web by its "caustic design," identifying these singular features arising from multi-stream flows as fundamental architectural elements that dictate the web's morphology. This provides a deep understanding of the topology and geometry of the dark matter web, moving beyond traditional density-based analyses. Furthermore, the work extends to crucial observational applications, demonstrating the utility of the Kinetic Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (kSZ) effect for precise peculiar velocity estimation. This technique provides a vital avenue for direct observational probes of the underlying large-scale dark matter flows, allowing for the validation of theoretical models. Collectively, this research provides a comprehensive and technically rigorous framework for analyzing and observing the intricate nature of dark matter distribution and its cosmological implications.

Emulation & Inference

The research area of Emulation and Inference addresses the critical need for computationally efficient and reliable models of complex physical systems. This body of work significantly advances the field by developing and applying advanced machine learning techniques, particularly Probabilistic Neural Networks (PNNs), to create high-fidelity, rapid surrogate models and reduced-order systems. The core innovation lies in leveraging PNNs to not only emulate complex phenomena at a fraction of the computational cost but also to provide robust and interpretable uncertainty quantification, which is crucial for scientific trustworthiness and decision-making. Methodologically, these contributions introduce sophisticated techniques such as differentiable predictions, enabling seamless integration into optimization and inverse problem frameworks for large-scale systems. The diverse applications span critical scientific and engineering domains, including the development of probabilistic neural network-based reduced-order models for fluid flow and structural mechanics. Furthermore, the research extends to high-impact areas like cosmology, exemplified by the Matter Power Spectrum Emulator for fR Modified Gravity. By providing fast, reliable, and uncertainty-aware predictions, this research enables accelerated design cycles, efficient parameter exploration, and high-throughput analysis, thereby fundamentally transforming scientific discovery and engineering innovation.
