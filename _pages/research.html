Foundation Models

Foundation Model research increasingly focuses on domain-specialization for scientific applications. Initial studies (AstroMLab 1) explored AI potential in astronomy Q&A. Later, AstroMLab 3 showed specialized 8B-parameter models can match GPT-4o performance in astronomy, while AstroMLab 4 achieved benchmark-topping results with a 70B-parameter reasoning model. This progress necessitates robust evaluation; EAIRA establishes methodologies for assessing AI as scientific research assistants. Collectively, these papers demonstrate how tailored AI models, rigorously evaluated, significantly enhance domain-specific knowledge access and reasoning, proving transformative for scientific inquiry.

Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning is revolutionizing scientific research by tackling complex data and enabling profound discoveries across diverse domains. Core methodologies include deep learning, generative adversarial networks, probabilistic modeling, and unsupervised learning. These are applied to enhance astronomical observations through image deconvolution, anomaly detection, and advanced galaxy morphology classification. ML further facilitates precise redshift estimation, detailed simulations of large-scale cosmic structures, and robust global field reconstruction from sparse sensor data. Crucially, the integration of interpretable uncertainty quantification and physical benchmarking ensures trustworthy AI, pushing the boundaries of discovery in fields like astrophysics, cosmology, and high energy physics.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Recent Dark Matter & Cosmology research employs diverse methods to map the Universe's large-scale structure. Cosmic shear analyses, like those using Hyper Suprime-Cam data, are crucial for constraining modified gravity theories such as f(R) gravity. Multi-stream approaches are key to understanding dark matter haloes, their internal substructure, and the intricate "caustic design" defining the cosmic web's topology and geometry. Large-scale surveys, including SPTpol's extended cluster survey, complement these efforts by characterizing galaxy populations and refining cosmological parameters. Tracing the cosmic web and understanding inner galaxy dynamics via stellar samples provides a comprehensive view of the dark matter skeleton and its evolution.

Emulation & Inference

Emulation and inference research employs advanced machine learning to accelerate scientific discovery in computationally intensive domains. Key methods involve constructing fast, accurate surrogate models using techniques such as probabilistic neural networks and Gaussian processes, often integrated with reduced-order modeling. This approach significantly enhances the efficiency of exploring complex parameter spaces and quantifying uncertainties. Applications span diverse fields, including cosmology, where it facilitates precise cluster mass estimation via weak lensing and the rapid generation of matter power spectra for modified gravity theories. Similarly, in fluid dynamics, these probabilistic surrogates enable swift predictions and robust data recovery, collectively accelerating scientific insight and model assessment.
