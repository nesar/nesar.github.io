Cosmology & Large Scale Structure Physics

My research focuses on understanding the universe's large-scale structure and constraining cosmological models. I develop and apply advanced methods, including cosmic shear analysis of Hyper Suprime-Cam data, to constrain $f(R)$ modified gravity. We extensively explore the dark matter cosmic web, utilizing a multistream view to characterize its caustic design, topology, and geometry, providing new insights into halo formation. I also leverage deep learning and neural networks to create differentiable predictions for large-scale structure, estimate peculiar velocities from the kinetic SZ effect, and develop matter power spectrum emulators. Our work refines our understanding of dark matter and gravity for current and future surveys.

Stellar & Galaxy Astronomy

My research focuses on advancing our understanding of stars and galaxies across cosmic scales. I develop machine learning pipelines for diverse applications, including detecting and modeling strong gravitational lenses and exploring galaxy morphology beyond traditional classifications. Our work also leverages synthetic spectra with machine learning for probabilistic redshift estimation, crucial for large surveys. I analyze extensive photometric samples of Red Clump stars from *Gaia* to map the Milky Way's structure and identify Carbon-Enhanced Metal-Poor star candidates using *Gaia* DR3 spectra, probing the early universe. We also optimize galaxy selection to reduce model error in weak lensing cluster mass estimations, enhancing our cosmological measurements.

Machine Learning Methods & Applications for Science

My research focuses on developing and applying cutting-edge machine learning methods to accelerate scientific discovery. I leverage deep learning, probabilistic neural networks, and generative models, including foundation models and GANs, for diverse applications. My work addresses critical challenges such as anomaly detection in astronomical images, high-dimensional field reconstruction, and building reduced-order surrogates for complex fluid flows and cosmological simulations. I also prioritize enhancing interpretability through statistically disentangled latent spaces and quantifying uncertainty in AI predictions. These efforts aim to create smart assistants, benchmark AI-evolved phenomena, and provide robust, interpretable tools for cosmology, astronomy, and high-energy physics.

Large Language Models & AI Scientific Assistants

My research focuses on developing and evaluating Large Language Models (LLMs) as effective scientific research assistants. I develop specialized LLMs, like the AstroMLab series for astronomy and models trained in spectroscopy, to imbue them with expert-level domain knowledge and advanced reasoning capabilities. For instance, my work demonstrates that even 8B-parameter specialized models can achieve GPT-4o level performance in complex astronomy Q&A, while larger 70B models can top established benchmarks. I also establish rigorous methodologies (EAIRA) for evaluating these AI models' utility in scientific research, assessing their ability to answer intricate queries and contribute to scientific discovery, thereby enhancing researchers' capabilities across diverse disciplines.
