Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning (ML) advances scientific research, particularly in astronomy and engineering. Key methods encompass deep learning, generative adversarial networks (GANs), probabilistic modeling, and unsupervised learning. Applications include enhancing interpretability via disentangled latent spaces, detecting anomalies in scientific images, and reconstructing global fields from sparse sensor data. ML models are crucial for galaxy morphology classification, strong gravitational lens detection, and accurate cosmological parameter estimation by reducing model error. Benchmarking AI-evolved physical structures and multi-task learning for sparse engineering data highlight ML's role in accelerating discovery, improving accuracy, and revealing new insights from complex scientific datasets.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Dark Matter and Cosmology research employs diverse methods to elucidate cosmic structure and evolution. Large-scale surveys, like Hyper Suprime-Cam and future SPHEREx data, constrain modified gravity (e.g., f(R)) using cosmic shear and galaxy cluster analyses. Multi-stream models and cosmological simulations (e.g., CRK-HACC) detail the intricate topology, geometry, and caustic features of dark matter halos and the cosmic web, informing galaxy formation. Galactic archaeology, utilizing Gaia's Red Clump stars, maps the Milky Way's dark matter halo. Advanced AI/ML techniques are increasingly vital for processing vast datasets from instruments like Rubin LSST, collectively advancing our understanding of dark matter, dark energy, and cosmic architecture.

Emulation & Inference

This research explores advanced emulation and inference techniques across cosmology, astrophysics, and fluid dynamics. Key methods include machine learning, deep neural networks, and particularly probabilistic neural networks and Gaussian process emulators, often serving as reduced-order surrogates. A central theme is robust uncertainty quantification, enabling probabilistic predictions for complex phenomena like cosmological subgrid models, large scale structure, modified gravity, and peculiar velocities. These approaches accelerate simulations, improve parameter estimation, and enhance data recovery for computationally intensive systems, offering interpretable and reliable insights into diverse physical processes from redshift estimation to fluid flow dynamics.
