Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning is revolutionizing scientific research, especially in astronomy, by addressing complex data challenges. Core methodologies encompass probabilistic modeling and automated frameworks for high-dimensional analysis, as seen in stress fields. Neural networks are vital for astronomical image processing, including point spread function deconvolution and generating synthetic spectra for probabilistic redshift estimation. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) excel in anomaly detection within galaxy images and exploring morphology. Modular deep learning pipelines enable robust strong gravitational lens detection. Furthermore, research prioritizes enhancing model interpretability through disentangled latent spaces and establishing best practices for impactful ML, driving new scientific discoveries.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Current Dark Matter and Cosmology research employs advanced simulations and large-scale observational surveys. Studies leverage sophisticated N-body simulations, often enhanced by AI benchmarking, to model galaxy formation and the intricate dark matter cosmic web, examining its topology, geometry, and multistream nature. These computational approaches provide detailed insights into dark matter halo evolution and large-scale structure formation. Concurrently, missions like SPHEREx, Hyper Suprime-Cam, and SPTpol gather vast observational data, enabling analyses such as cosmic shear to constrain cosmological parameters and alternative gravity theories like f(R) gravity. This integrated approach is crucial for understanding the universe's large-scale structure and the elusive properties of dark matter.

Emulation & Inference

Emulation and inference research applies advanced machine learning, via neural and probabilistic networks. High-fidelity emulators and reduced-order surrogates accelerate simulations and parameter space exploration for complex systems. Deep learning inference refines estimations from sparse observational data, such as astrophysical cluster masses. Crucially, interpretable uncertainty quantification enhances model reliability, reduces error, and provides robust predictive capabilities across cosmology, fluid dynamics, and high-energy physics.
