Machine Learning for Science

Machine learning is revolutionizing scientific research, particularly in astrophysics and cosmology. Key methods include deep learning, generative adversarial networks (GANs), and probabilistic modeling. These techniques are applied across diverse challenges such as anomaly detection in astronomical images, high-dimensional stress field analysis, and global field reconstruction from sparse sensors. Researchers utilize generative models for synthetic data generation, enhancing interpretability through disentangled latent spaces, and benchmarking AI-evolved physical phenomena like the cosmic web. Further applications encompass multi-task learning for engineering, automated strong gravitational lens detection, peculiar velocity estimation, and robust redshift estimation, ultimately accelerating discovery and improving our understanding of complex scientific phenomena.

Dark Matter & Cosmology

Recent Dark Matter & Cosmology research integrates diverse methods to illuminate cosmic structure and its fundamental constituents. Large-scale observational surveys, like Hyper Suprime-Cam and SPTpol, are employed to constrain modified gravity theories and map the cosmic web and galaxy clusters, with future missions such as SPHEREx promising new data. Cosmological simulations are vital for dissecting the dark matter web's intricate multistream topology and caustic design, and the fine-grained structure of dark matter haloes, increasingly leveraging generative AI models. Additionally, photometric and spectroscopic analyses of Milky Way stars, including red clump stars and carbon-enhanced metal-poor candidates, trace galactic formation and chemical evolution. These combined efforts collectively refine cosmological models and deepen our understanding of dark matter's pervasive influence.

Emulation & Inference

Research in emulation and inference leverages advanced machine learning techniques to accelerate scientific discovery and enhance data analysis across domains like cosmology, fluid dynamics, and high-energy physics. Key methods include constructing high-fidelity emulators and surrogates, often employing Gaussian Processes or Probabilistic Neural Networks, to mimic computationally expensive simulations. These models enable efficient inference, parameter estimation, and exploration of complex physics, such as cosmological subgrid models or modified gravity theories. A central focus is interpretable uncertainty quantification, crucial for reliable predictions and robust decision-making. By reducing model error and enabling differentiable predictions, this work significantly advances our capability to analyze large datasets and gain deeper insights from complex systems.
