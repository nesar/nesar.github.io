Cosmic Structure & Cosmology

Nesar S Ramachandra's research extensively investigates cosmic structure and cosmology, employing diverse methodologies. A significant focus involves characterizing the dark matter web and haloes through multistream, topological, and caustic analyses, enhancing our understanding of structure formation. Furthermore, the work constrains modified gravity theories, such as $f(R)$ models, using techniques like $k$-cut cosmic shear analysis and matter power spectrum emulation. Contributions also include optimizing weak lensing cluster mass estimation for surveys like SPTpol by refined galaxy selection. A prominent theme is the application of advanced machine learning and deep neural networks (e.g., SHAMNet) for differentiable predictions, peculiar velocity estimation, and benchmarking AI-generated cosmic web, significantly advancing large-scale structure analysis.

AI/ML Methodologies & Scientific Computing

Nesar S Ramachandra's research centers on developing advanced AI/ML methodologies for scientific computing. A significant focus involves probabilistic neural networks and reduced-order models, enabling efficient and robust surrogate modeling for complex phenomena like fluid flows and facilitating global field reconstruction from sparse sensor data. The work critically addresses interpretable uncertainty quantification, enhancing the reliability and trustworthiness of AI applications, particularly in fields such as High Energy Physics. Furthermore, research delves into disentangled latent spaces in generative models for improved interpretability in scientific datasets, alongside establishing frameworks for evaluating AI's role as scientific research assistants. This body of work significantly advances AI's utility for scientific discovery and complex system analysis.

Astronomical Image & Spectral Analysis with AI

Nesar S Ramachandra's research employs advanced AI techniques for astronomical image and spectral analysis. Neural networks are utilized for point spread function deconvolution, significantly enhancing image clarity. Machine learning, specifically with synthetic spectra, enables robust probabilistic redshift estimation (SYTH-Z), vital for cosmological studies. Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are developed for anomaly detection in galaxy images, facilitating the discovery of unusual astronomical phenomena. Unsupervised machine learning explores galaxy morphology beyond traditional classifications, providing new insights into galaxy evolution. Additionally, deep learning pipelines are designed for efficient detection and modeling of strong gravitational lenses. This body of work substantially advances data interpretation, automation, and discovery capabilities in astronomy.

Galactic & Stellar Astronomy, and Astronomical LLMs

Nesar S Ramachandra's research bridges Galactic & Stellar Astronomy with Astronomical Large Language Models (LLMs). Stellar studies employ vast photometric and spectroscopic data, for instance, analyzing 2.6 million Red Clump stars to map the Milky Way and Gaia DR3 spectra to identify Carbon-Enhanced Metal-Poor stars, enhancing stellar population understanding. For LLMs, the work develops domain-specialized models (8B to 70B parameters) achieving benchmark-topping performance in astronomy Q&A, often matching or exceeding commercial models. Key methods include fine-tuning LLMs to interpret spectroscopy, demonstrating significant advancements in AI's ability to reason with complex astronomical data, enhancing research accessibility and efficiency.
