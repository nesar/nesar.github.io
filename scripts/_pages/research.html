SZ-Selected Galaxy Cluster Surveys & Catalogs

Nesar S. Ramachandra's research prominently features the utilization of the Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) effect for robustly detecting and cataloging galaxy clusters. Employing data from major cosmological surveys, particularly the South Pole Telescope (SPT) and the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT), this work demonstrates the power of SZ-selected samples. The SPT-SZ survey, for example, yielded a significant cluster sample spanning redshifts 0.05 to 1.35, while the ACT survey produced a comprehensive catalog from a decade of observations. These meticulously constructed catalogs provide mass-selected samples critical for advancing precision cosmology, enabling stringent tests of cosmological parameters, dark energy models, and the growth of cosmic structure.

Cosmological Constraints from Cluster Abundance

Nesar S Ramachandra's research focuses on constraining cosmology via galaxy cluster abundance derived from large-area Sunyaev-Zel'dovich (SZ) surveys. A key method involves analyzing the cluster redshift distribution, as demonstrated with the SPT-SZ survey. Furthermore, the cluster mass function is utilized, exemplified by the ACT DR4 catalog's precise measurement of σ₈, the amplitude of matter fluctuations. These studies critically depend on accurately calibrated cluster catalogs and their mass-observable relations. The work provides powerful, independent tests of the standard cosmological model, offering vital insights into dark energy and cosmic structure growth.

High-Redshift Extragalactic Source Characterization

Nesar S Ramachandra’s research characterizes high-redshift extragalactic sources using millimeter-wave observations from instruments like the South Pole Telescope (SPT) and Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT). A primary method involves searching for gravitationally lensed galaxies with SPT, leveraging cosmic magnification to detect and study faint, distant sources. Concurrently, ACT data is utilized to derive photometric redshifts for millimeter-selected galaxies, providing essential distance estimates for large statistical samples. These techniques enable the precise localization and characterization of obscured, highly star-forming galaxies in the early universe. The research’s impact includes building robust catalogs of distant sources, constraining models of galaxy evolution, and enhancing understanding of star formation in the nascent cosmos.

Cosmic Microwave Background & General Cosmological Parameter Analysis

Research, notably from the Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) and Planck 2018 results, meticulously analyzes Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB) anisotropies to constrain fundamental cosmological parameters. Key methods involve comprehensive likelihood analyses of CMB power spectra, leveraging data from both ground-based (ACT) and space-based (Planck) experiments. This approach rigorously tests and refines the standard six-parameter Lambda-CDM cosmological model. The impact is profound: these studies have established the most precise determination of the universe's composition, age, and expansion history. Moreover, they highlight significant tensions, like the Hubble constant discrepancy, driving further essential investigations in precision cosmology.
