Machine learning has rapidly become an indispensable tool across diverse scientific disciplines, particularly in astrophysics, where modern observatories generate unprecedented volumes of complex data. The sheer scale and intricate nature of these datasets necessitate advanced computational techniques to extract meaningful insights, identify subtle patterns, and accelerate the pace of discovery. Machine learning offers powerful solutions for tasks ranging from enhancing image fidelity and classifying celestial objects to simulating complex cosmic structures and detecting rare astrophysical phenomena that might otherwise elude traditional analysis methods.

The application of machine learning in astronomy addresses fundamental challenges, such as overcoming instrumental limitations, efficiently processing vast data streams, and interpreting the underlying physical processes encoded within observations. By leveraging sophisticated algorithms, researchers can push the boundaries of what is observable and understandable, enabling new frontiers in our exploration of the universe. This transformative approach is pivotal for handling the "big data" paradigm in science, allowing for the automated recognition of features, the discovery of novel astrophysical sources, and the validation of theoretical models against observational evidence.

My research focuses on developing cutting-edge machine learning solutions to address critical challenges in astrophysics, advancing our ability to analyze complex astronomical data and uncover new scientific insights. I have developed a novel approach using Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for Anomaly Detection in Astronomical Images. This method allows for the robust identification of unusual celestial objects or transient events that deviate from expected patterns, which is crucial for discovering previously unknown phenomena in vast sky surveys.

Furthermore, my work on Neural Network Based Point Spread Function Deconvolution For Astronomical Applications significantly improves the clarity and resolution of astronomical observations. By leveraging deep learning architectures, I have enabled the recovery of finer details in images, enhancing the detectability of faint and distant sources that are otherwise blurred by the telescope's optics. To ensure the trustworthiness and scientific rigor of AI-generated data, I led the development of a framework for Physical Benchmarking for AI-Generated Cosmic Web. This methodology rigorously validates the physical consistency of synthetic cosmic structures generated by AI models, ensuring their reliability and applicability for cosmological studies. Finally, I designed and implemented A Modular Deep Learning Pipeline for Galaxy-Scale Strong Gravitational Lens Detection. This efficient pipeline automates the identification of rare gravitational lensing events in large survey datasets, which are vital probes for understanding dark matter distribution and cosmic expansion.

Collectively, my contributions demonstrate the transformative power of advanced machine learning techniques in pushing the boundaries of astrophysical research. From enhancing observational data quality and ensuring the physical fidelity of simulations to automating the detection of rare and significant cosmic phenomena, my work provides innovative tools that enable scientists to extract deeper insights from the universe, accelerating the pace of astronomical discovery.
